Background & method

Potential = free roof area × roof share × 0.18 kWp/m² × kWh per kWp. Each 0.1° cell gets one figure per roof-size band. It answers where to look for future rooftop solar. It does not measure PV that exists, and it is not a forecast of what will be built.

Building size

Every VIDA Open Buildings footprint of at least 25 m², which at this roof share carries about 1.5 kWp. A footprint is not a suitable roof: structure, orientation, shade and ownership are not modelled, so weak and informal roofs count here like any other.

Lack of PV

A roof counts as free by one minus its roof-classifier score. A roof touching a hand-mapped OpenStreetMap solar outline, or a model outline that earns capacity, counts as taken. A roof carrying PV counts as fully taken, however little of it the panels cover, so Already in use is the share of roof area on such roofs, not panel coverage. The score also responds to the kind of roof that adopts PV, so a large industrial roof loses some potential it may still have. Its level is unreviewed in sparse cells, and outside the building-density band it was calibrated over the Already in use view leaves cells uncoloured rather than show an unmeasured figure. Potential keeps those cells, because the score barely moves a figure that is mostly roof area.

Roof share

The share of a footprint that mapped rooftop PV covers on the roofs that carry it, from the calibration areas, weighted by area. The map means: what if every free roof took PV the way PV roofs do today. It is not an engineering maximum. The low and high figures use the median PV roof and the 90th-percentile one; potential scales directly with this one number, and it is the largest uncertainty on the page.

Sunshine

PVGIS-ERA5 modelled yield for 2020: a fixed, equator-facing array tilted at the latitude, with 14% system losses, interpolated from a coarse grid of probes. It is one modelled year on a coarse grid, so local haze and terrain shading are not resolved. Sunshine moves potential far less than roof area does.
