Pakistan's solar, counted three times over

The same country, the same imagery, three different standards of proof. Verified counts only PV a person has drawn in OpenStreetMap plus the small rooftops where two independent detectors agree. Best estimate adds the satellite model's own recall-corrected detections and its per-building density estimate: the highest figure this project is willing to defend. Ceiling swaps the small-PV side for a much looser national assumption — every roofclf-flagged building nationwide at a flat, un-tuned precision weight, not the calibrated per-cell estimate — and adds every large installation already known on top: a bound built on a cruder assumption, not a tighter measurement. The gap between the three is the honest measure of how much of this is still unresolved.

How to read these numbers
What each tier admits as evidence Verified / Best estimate / Ceiling, defined+
Verified nothing here rests on a single model
Every PV installation drawn by a person in OpenStreetMap (0 of them, 0 km² of panel and site area), plus the sub-400 m² buildings where two independent instruments agree — roofclf, a per-building size and reflectance classifier, and SPPI, a zero-training spectral index. Requiring agreement raises measured precision from 0.55 to 0.62 on held-out hand-mapped quadrats, at the cost of recall. This is a floor and it is known to be one: OpenStreetMap coverage of Pakistan is incomplete and uneven, and the small-PV term only applies inside the checked cells.
Best estimate this project's own pick
The hand-mapped population plus the satellite model's own ≥400 m² detections, precision-calibrated and recall-corrected, plus the roofclf per-building density estimate inside the same checked cells. The model's detections and the OSM mapping overlap heavily, so the overlap is removed rather than added twice (see the next section). This is the highest number the project defends; it is still incomplete, because the small-PV term covers only about a fifth of national buildings.
Ceiling a bound, not an estimate
A much looser assumption on the small-PV side, plus every large installation already known. Every roofclf-flagged building nationwide (threshold 0.3064, the same cut used to train the classifier) is credited at a flat 0.5 precision weight — not its own per-building probability, not restricted to the density-checked cells — after excluding buildings that already sit near a known ≥400 m² detection, so the two terms don't overlap. The large-PV side is the same recall-corrected total used everywhere else on this page, across every placement. The small-PV side is explicitly uncalibrated at national scale: a flat weight known not to survive the shift from nine urban/industrial calibration quadrats to the country's mostly rural buildings. Treat this tier as an outer bound on plausibility, not a measurement.
How double counting is avoided the mapped and the detected are largely the same installations+

OpenStreetMap and the satellite detector are looking at the same country, so naively adding them would count many installations twice. Each model detection carries the identity of the OSM feature it matched, if any: 0 of the 0 hand-mapped installations were also found by the model, and those detections already carry the OSM geometry. The Best estimate therefore adds only the 0 MWp of hand-mapped PV the model never found, on top of the model's own recall-corrected total.

Two residual overlaps are known and small. The recall correction is itself an estimate of what the model missed, so adding measured misses on top double counts that correction — but nationally the correction only adds 0.8% to the calibrated total, so the effect is negligible here. Separately, small OSM rooftop installations may sit on buildings the roofclf term also flags; nationally all sub-400 m² OSM features together amount to under 200 MWp, so this is on the order of 1% of the Best estimate.

The Ceiling tier avoids the same overlap by construction rather than by subtraction: its small-PV component only includes buildings with no existing ≥400 m² detection within 30 m (the "incremental" population roofclf_capacity.incremental_capacity already computes), so adding the known large-PV total on top does not double count either.

Where the small-PV numbers apply reading the dashed outline on the map+

Installations under 400 m² are below the satellite detector's practical floor at 10 m resolution, so they are estimated per building instead. That estimate is only reported inside the dashed outline: the 0 cells whose building density falls inside the range covered by the hand-mapped calibration quadrats. Outside it, small PV reads as zero in the Verified and Best estimate tiers — not because small solar is absent there, but because there is no calibration evidence either way. Those cells hold roughly a fifth of the country's buildings and concentrate in its largest cities, so both tiers understate the national small-PV total by an unknown but certainly positive amount.

The Ceiling tier deliberately ignores this restriction, along with every other one.

External checks, outside this pipeline two independent, non-imagery anchors+

Two administrative and trade data points, neither derived from this pipeline, bracket the ladder from outside:

FigureValueScope

NEPRA net-metering (Pakistan's regulator, the closest available analogue to Germany's MaStR register): 5.3 GW registered nationally by April 2025 across 283,000 consumers, average size 18.7 kWp — far below this project's 400 m² detection floor, so the register is dominated by exactly the small rooftops the detector cannot see. It is a floor, not a total: unregistered, self-consumption and off-grid installations never appear in it.

Chinese customs panel exports put Pakistan's 2024 imports at 16.91 GW and roughly 50 GW cumulative by August 2025 — a loose order-of-magnitude ceiling, since it is import volume rather than installed capacity. Both the Best estimate and the Ceiling sit between the two anchors, with the Ceiling closer to the customs figure — that proximity is a coincidence of the flat precision weight chosen, not evidence the Ceiling is calibrated; its own components are still an explicitly uncalibrated national extrapolation, not a measurement.

Why the orientation plot only fills part of the circle a sensor limit, not a property of Pakistani rooftops+

Sentinel-2 crosses this latitude at a fixed ~10:30 local time, so across two full years of imagery the sun's azimuth at the moment of every overpass never once swings west of due south. A panel facing southwest, west, or north cannot glint into this sensor no matter how long you wait — that is a limit of the satellite's orbit, not evidence about how rooftops in Pakistan are actually oriented. The shaded wedge marks orientations this survey cannot observe at all; nothing is drawn there because nothing was measured there.

A further 0% of ≥1,000 m² installations glint only once (or on dates that disagree on a panel geometry), so they are correctly flagged as "PV is probably here" but carry no orientation information; another 0% show no glint signal in either year.

Data & methods what's under the hood+

Imagery is Sentinel-2 L2A, dry-season composites. Hand-mapped PV is the national OpenStreetMap solar pull, areas measured geodesically, converted at 0.18 kWp/m² for rooftop module area and 0.07 kWp/m² for ground-mount site area. Detections ≥400 m² come from a TerraMind geospatial foundation model fine-tuned for segmentation, trained on Germany and inferred on Pakistan, then precision-calibrated against a glint-based validation study and recall-corrected. Sub-400 m² capacity comes from two per-building instruments (roofclf, a size and reflectance classifier; SPPI, a zero-training spectral index) trained and validated on hand-mapped 1 km² quadrats, restricted to cells whose building density matches those quadrats (Verified and Best estimate), or applied nationally at a flat precision weight explicitly known to be uncalibrated at that scale (Ceiling). Panel orientation comes from fitting a fixed tilt and azimuth to specular sun-glint spikes across two years of imagery, checked for self-consistency across independent dates. Building footprints are VIDA Open Buildings. Full derivations and caveats live in this project's methodology notes (docs/methods/density.md).
