Background & method

Potential is not a capacity measurement of existing PV, and it does not use any PV-presence probability. It starts from every building footprint ≥ 200 m² nationwide (from the same per-building roof-classifier scoring run that also predicts PV presence elsewhere in this project, but here only its roof_area_m2 column is used — pure building geometry, not a prediction). From each cell's total large-roof area, the model's own probability-weighted expected PV area is subtracted (an upper-leaning estimate of what's already covered, so a roof with any sub-threshold PV signal is conservatively excluded from "opportunity" rather than double-suggested) to get an uncovered large-roof area. That area converts to peak capacity at the same 0.18 kWp/m² module constant used throughout this project, then to annual energy via PVGIS-modelled specific yield (kWh/kWp/yr) interpolated from a coarse, cached probe grid across the country — irradiance is smooth at country scale, so a few dozen points are enough.

Why 200 m² and not the model's 400 m² detection floor

The segmentation model that finds existing PV is trained with everything below 400 m² burned as "ignore," so it has essentially no discriminating signal in the 200–400 m² band. That band's "potential" therefore reads as almost entirely uncovered regardless of whether PV is actually there — expected, not a bug, and worth knowing when comparing this map to the 400 m² candidate population reported elsewhere on this site. 200 m² was chosen deliberately to reach further into the realistic rooftop-opportunity space (roughly where Germany's MaStR register shows most rooftop capacity actually sits), not to restate the already-known ≥400 m² population.

Saturation

This tab adds no new computation: it is the same per-cell/per-region PV-area-over-roof-area ratio the density stage always computes, drawn as its own choropleth so dense-adoption urban areas are visually distinct from under-adopted ones, rather than only appearing in a table.
