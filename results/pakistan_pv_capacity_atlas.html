Small-PV supplement (< 400 m²) — 93 cells only

The segmentation model burns everything below the training floor as ignore, so it has no sensitivity below 400 m² at all — recall correction cannot rescue a population the model never sees. This figure comes from a different instrument (roofclf per-building scoring + a building-density domain restriction to the settlement-density range the 8 calibration quadrats actually cover) and intentionally stops at those 93 cells rather than extrapolating.

Do not rescale this number. 6,628 MWp describes only the 93 cells shown here (2.1% of national cells, 19.1% of national buildings). Extrapolating by that share to estimate a country total implies ~315 GWp — the exact base-rate-transfer failure this module was built to avoid. The other ~81% of buildings are mostly rural and not known to share this rate; per-quadrat measured adoption ranges 0.235–4.833× the model's own prediction across nine calibration quadrats.

Methodology notes

Are OSM PV labels the model misses added directly to the capacity total?
No. OSM geometry replacement (postprocess.replace_with_osm_geometry) only corrects the shape of a candidate the model already detected within 30 m of a mapped installation — it never creates a new candidate from an OSM-mapped installation the model produced no nearby detection for. A real, OSM-mapped installation with zero nearby model output contributes zero area to pv_area_det_*/pv_area_cal_*.
So how does the estimate account for installations the model missed?
Statistically, not additively. Mapped OSM installations serve as a held-out reference to measure the model's recall per size bin (what share of real, mapped installations get matched by any candidate). est_mwp_rc — the headline number above — then scales every surviving candidate by 1 / recall(bin), a Horvitz–Thompson estimator. It assumes missed installations in a bin resemble detected ones in that bin; it does not sum any specific missed installation's own mapped area.
Why doesn't that fix the sub-400 m² population?
Because recall correction only rescales what the model can, in principle, detect. Below 400 m² the segmentation model was trained with those arrays burned as ignore, so its recall there is effectively zero by construction — 1/recall × ~0 ≈ 0. That's why the small-PV figure above comes from an entirely separate instrument rather than a correction factor on the same one.
Why are the two figures reported separately instead of summed?
Different populations, different coverage, different confidence. The large-PV figure is a national, recall-corrected estimate over a population the model can see. The small-PV figure is a bounded, non-extrapolated measurement over 19.1% of buildings. Summing 5,078 + 6,628 would silently imply the second number is national when it explicitly is not.
