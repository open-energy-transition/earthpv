Pakistan's solar, counted three times over

The same country, the same imagery, three different standards of proof. Verified counts only PV a person has drawn in OpenStreetMap plus the small rooftops where two independent detectors agree. Best adds the satellite model's own recall-corrected detections and its per-building density estimate: the highest figure this project is willing to defend. Ceiling swaps the small-PV side for a much looser national assumption and adds every large installation already known on top: a bound built on a cruder assumption, not a tighter measurement. This is a research methodology under active validation, not a finished census — see “How confident should you be in this?” below for what's independently corroborated and what's still open.

Background
How to read these numbers
How confident should you be in this? preliminary results, sampling caveats, independent corroboration+

Read this as promising preliminary results from an ongoing methodology, not a finished capacity census. What's genuinely novel here — a reproducible pipeline using free satellite imagery and open-source geospatial AI to estimate distributed solar deployment where official statistics are sparse — is true regardless of whether any single number on this page holds up exactly. Treat the numbers as the current state of an experiment being actively tested, not a settled fact.

The calibration quadrats are hand-picked, not randomly sampled. All 12 ground-truth quadrats behind the small-PV instruments were chosen by a researcher to cover a spread of landscape types (planned housing, dense informal urban, industrial, arid/bare-land), not drawn at random from a defined national frame. Only 4 of them have been through a full human completeness pass strict enough to trust their negatives (see the teal markers on the map). Purposive sampling at any size — whether this many quadrats or several times more — does not by itself support a formal national margin of error; that would need a probability sample drawn from the national building-density frame, which does not yet exist. More quadrats have real value (each new one has surfaced a genuinely new failure mode so far), but count alone does not resolve this.

Independent, non-imagery data points land in the same order of magnitude. Pakistan's NEPRA net-metering register — a government administrative record with no connection to this pipeline — puts registered rooftop solar at 5.3-6.3 GW nationally (a floor, since it only counts customers who completed formal registration paperwork). Chinese customs export data separately puts cumulative panel imports into Pakistan at roughly 50 GW by mid-2025, a much looser ceiling on the whole market, utility-scale included. This page's Verified and Best tiers sit inside that bracket, and its Ceiling sits a further step above it — two independent, non-satellite data sources landing in a mutually consistent range is real corroboration for the order of magnitude, even though it cannot confirm any single number here precisely.

What each tier admits as evidence Verified / Best estimate / Ceiling, defined+
Verified nothing here rests on a single model
Every PV installation drawn by a person in OpenStreetMap (0 of them), plus the sub-400 m² buildings where two independent instruments agree — roofclf, a per-building size and reflectance classifier, and SPPI, a zero-training spectral index. Requiring agreement raises measured precision on held-out hand-mapped quadrats, at the cost of recall. This is a floor and it is known to be one: OpenStreetMap coverage is incomplete and uneven, and the small-PV term only applies inside the checked cells.
Best estimate this project's own pick
The hand-mapped population plus the satellite model's own ≥400 m² detections, precision-calibrated and recall-corrected, plus the roofclf per-building density estimate inside the same checked cells. The model's detections and the OSM mapping overlap heavily, so the overlap is removed rather than added twice (see the next section). This is the highest number the project defends; it is still incomplete, because the small-PV term covers only a fraction of national buildings.
Ceiling a bound, not an estimate
A much looser assumption on the small-PV side, plus every large installation already known. Every roofclf-flagged building nationwide (the same threshold used to train the classifier) is credited at a flat precision weight — not its own per-building probability, not restricted to the density-checked cells — after excluding buildings that already sit near a known ≥400 m² detection, so the two terms don't overlap. The large-PV side is the same recall-corrected total used everywhere else on this page, across every placement. The small-PV side is explicitly uncalibrated at national scale: a flat weight known not to survive the shift from a handful of urban and industrial calibration quadrats to the country's mostly rural buildings. Treat this tier as an outer bound on plausibility, not a measurement.
How double counting is avoided the mapped and the detected are largely the same installations+

OpenStreetMap and the satellite detector are looking at the same country, so naively adding them would count many installations twice. Each model detection carries the identity of the OSM feature it matched, if any: 0 of the 0 hand-mapped installations were also found by the model, and those detections already carry the OSM geometry. The Best estimate therefore adds only the 0 MWp of hand-mapped PV the model never found, on top of the model's own recall-corrected total.

The Ceiling tier avoids the same overlap by construction rather than by subtraction: its small-PV component only includes buildings with no existing ≥400 m² detection within 30 m (the "incremental" population roofclf_capacity.incremental_capacity already computes), so adding the known large-PV total on top does not double count either.

Where the small-PV numbers apply reading the dashed outline on the map+

Installations under 400 m² are below the satellite detector's practical floor at 10 m resolution, so they are estimated per building instead. That estimate is only reported inside the dashed outline: the 0 cells whose building density falls inside the range covered by the hand-mapped calibration quadrats. Outside it, small PV reads as zero in the Verified and Best estimate tiers — not because small solar is absent there, but because there is no calibration evidence either way. The Ceiling tier deliberately ignores this restriction, along with every other one.

Data & methods what's under the hood+

Imagery is Sentinel-2 L2A, dry-season composites. Hand-mapped PV is the national OpenStreetMap solar pull, areas measured geodesically, converted at 0.18 kWp/m² for rooftop module area and the land constant for everything else. Detections ≥400 m² come from a TerraMind geospatial foundation model fine-tuned for segmentation, trained on Germany and inferred on this AOI, then precision-calibrated and recall-corrected. Sub-400 m² capacity comes from two per-building instruments (roofclf, a size and reflectance classifier; SPPI, a zero-training spectral index) trained and validated on hand-mapped 1 km² quadrats, restricted to cells whose building density matches those quadrats (Verified and Best estimate), or applied nationally at a flat precision weight explicitly known to be uncalibrated at that scale (Ceiling). Building footprints are VIDA Open Buildings. Full derivations and caveats live in this project's methodology notes (docs/methods/density.md).
