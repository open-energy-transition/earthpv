How to read it

Image cube. The top layer is the mapped panels and roofs, drawn at 1 m. Below it sits the share of each 10 m pixel the panels cover, then the ten bands from blue (492 nm) down to shortwave infrared (2,202 nm). The six bands that are 20 m at the sensor show as 2 by 2 blocks. Click a pixel to drill a column through every layer, or select an array in the list to outline it on all of them. Peel lifts layers off so any band can be seen from above.

Spectral space. The same pixels, moved to a space whose axes are three bands of your choice, measured as contrast to the roofs around them. The amber line is where panels push a pixel as their cover grows from none to all of it; the blue ellipses show how far PV-free roofs already scatter. A pixel only stands out once it leaves them.

Inspector. The selected pixel's spectrum against nearby roofs, its PV cover on the 10 m and the 20 m grid, and whether a score combining the bands clears two standard deviations of roof-to-roof variation.
