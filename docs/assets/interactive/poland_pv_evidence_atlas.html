What is PV glint and how is it detected?

PV Glint is a brief, brighter-than-diffuse flash where a panel's specular reflection lines up with the sun and Sentinel-2's fixed overpass geometry on one date -- see the method page for the full derivation and an annotated image gallery. A single spike can't be checked for self-consistency -- 0% of installations glint once (or on dates that disagree on a panel geometry) and so are correctly detected as "PV is probably here" but carry no orientation information.

Why the plot only fills part of the circle

Sentinel-2 crosses this latitude at a fixed ~10:30 local time, so in every image of the survey window the sun's azimuth at the moment of overpass never once swings west of due south -- a panel facing southwest, west, or north cannot glint into this sensor no matter how long you wait. That's a sensor limit, not a property of rooftops here: the shaded wedge (0° wide) marks orientations this survey cannot observe at all, not orientations known to be empty. Nothing is plotted there, because nothing was measured there.

Background
How to read these numbers
How the estimate is built the methods in plain language, what each can see, how they add up+
How confident should you be in this? preliminary results, sampling caveats, independent corroboration+

Best estimate: 7,781 MWp, with a 90% range of 6,411–10,398 MWp. The range covers two measured sources of uncertainty, not everything that could move this number. Below: what is inside the range, and what is not.

Inside the range:

  • The two area-to-power constants. Panel area on a roof converts at about 0.18 kWp per m², the rating of a typical module; the outline of a ground-mounted park converts at about 0.05 kWp per m², measured on parks whose capacity is known. Each is varied over its own range (90%: 0.15–0.21 and 0.035–0.075 kWp per m²).
  • How reliable the segmentation model's detections are. How often its detections turn out to be real, and how many mapped arrays it misses, were measured size class by size class against mapped installations. Those measurements have their own sampling error, which is drawn into the range.

Outside the range, and not something more arithmetic can add back:

  • No area of this country has been exhaustively mapped by hand to check these numbers against. The detectors' measured accuracy is carried over from the countries where it was measured, and how well it transfers here has not itself been measured. That is a different kind of uncertainty from the range above, and no amount of arithmetic converts one into the other.
  • The small-installation half of this page does not exist yet. No area of Poland has been mapped panel by panel, so there is nothing to calibrate a per-building rooftop classifier against here. Everything on the map is either hand-mapped in OpenStreetMap or an array the segmentation model outlined directly, which in practice means installations of roughly 400 m² and up. The household rooftop systems behind most of Poland's solar growth are mostly below that size.
  • Imagery covers where people live, plus every mapped solar site. Map cells were imaged if they hold at least 1,000 buildings or any hand-mapped solar installation. A utility-scale park built on empty land that nobody has mapped yet falls outside both rules and is not on this page.
  • Solar parks the model outlines but nobody has mapped are not counted. A model outline above 100,000 m² only counts when it carries a mapped plant geometry, which removes large false detections and, with them, the occasional real park that is not yet in OpenStreetMap. Mapping such a park brings it into the verified layer.

Treat this as an early estimate from an active research pipeline, not a census. What it offers is a reproducible way to estimate distributed solar from free satellite imagery and open-source models where official statistics are thin or missing. Expect the figures to move as the evidence behind them grows.

Nothing on this page rests on hand-mapped ground truth from this country. Exhaustively mapped areas -- every panel in a few square kilometres, drawn by hand -- are what turns a detection into a capacity estimate: they measure how much of a flagged roof is actually covered in panels, and how many real installations the detectors miss entirely. Until some exist here, read these figures accordingly. Mapping one is the most useful thing anyone can contribute.

Using the map and exporting figures layers, your own data, downloads, licences+

Layers. "Show" picks the quantity the cells are coloured by: the Best estimate, the OSM-mapped capacity alone, the satellite detections above 400 m², rooftop PV below it, farm ground PV below it (arrays beside fields, where the farm detector has run), or the gap between the Best estimate and what is already mapped, which is where mapping effort pays off most. Each cell shows its total in MWp. The colour scale is logarithmic and is refitted to whichever quantity is shown; the legend always states its range.

Your own data. "Layers" → "Add a layer" overlays a GeoJSON file, a CSV with latitude/longitude columns, a GeoJSON URL, an XYZ raster tile URL or a WMS (use {bbox-epsg-3857} in its GetMap URL). Files are read in your browser and are not uploaded anywhere. The live OpenStreetMap overlay queries the public Overpass API for the area in view when you zoom in, so it shows mapping done after this page was built.

Exports. Every figure downloads as PNG (for slides), SVG (vector, for papers) and CSV (the numbers behind it); the map as PNG with its legend and attribution, and the grid as GeoJSON or CSV. Basemap imagery and tiles keep their own providers' terms: OpenStreetMap data is © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), the grey canvas and the satellite imagery © Esri and its partners.
