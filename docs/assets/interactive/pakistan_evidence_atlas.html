How to read these numbers
How confident should you be in this? preliminary results, sampling caveats, independent corroboration+

Both headline figures carry a 90% range, and it is wide. Verified is 6,139 MWp with a 90% range of 5,096–7,498; Best estimate is 16,441 MWp with a range of 12,883–19,147. Four things are inside those ranges: the two area-to-capacity constants (how many kWp a square metre of panel, or of solar-farm site, actually carries); the segmentation model's own measured precision and recall by installation size; the roof-coverage ratio's sensitivity to which ground-truth quadrats were mapped, measured by resampling the quadrats themselves rather than the buildings inside them; and, for the small-PV contribution in cells with no nearby calibration at all, an explicit and deliberately wide allowance for extrapolating a city-calibrated relationship onto rural roofs. Two things are not inside them, and no arithmetic can put them there: the quadrats were hand-picked rather than randomly sampled, so this is not a formal national margin of error, and ground-truth completeness is relative to the date of the mapping imagery rather than the satellite composite, which biases the measured precision downward by an unknown amount.

Read this as promising preliminary results from an ongoing methodology, not a finished capacity census. What's genuinely novel here — a reproducible pipeline using free satellite imagery and open-source geospatial AI to estimate distributed solar deployment where official statistics are sparse — is true regardless of whether any single number on this page holds up exactly. Treat the numbers as the current state of an experiment being actively tested, not a settled fact.

The calibration quadrats are hand-picked, not randomly sampled. All 23 ground-truth quadrats behind the small-PV instruments were chosen by a researcher to cover a spread of landscape types (planned housing, dense informal urban, industrial, arid/bare-land), not drawn at random from a defined national frame. Only 23 of them have been through a full human completeness pass strict enough to trust their negatives (see the teal markers on the map). Purposive sampling at any size — whether this many quadrats or several times more — does not by itself support a formal national margin of error; that would need a probability sample drawn from the national building-density frame, which does not yet exist. More quadrats have real value (each new one has surfaced a genuinely new failure mode so far), but count alone does not resolve this.

Independent, non-imagery data points land in the same order of magnitude. Pakistan's NEPRA net-metering register — a government administrative record with no connection to this pipeline — puts registered rooftop solar at 5.3-6.3 GW nationally (a floor, since it only counts customers who completed formal registration paperwork). Chinese customs export data separately puts cumulative panel imports into Pakistan at roughly 50 GW by mid-2025, a much looser ceiling on the whole market, utility-scale included. This page's Verified and Best tiers sit inside that bracket — two independent, non-satellite data sources landing in a mutually consistent range is real corroboration for the order of magnitude, even though it cannot confirm any single number here precisely.

What each tier admits as evidence Verified / Best estimate, defined+
Verified nothing here rests on a single model
Every PV installation drawn by a person in OpenStreetMap (0 of them), plus the sub-400 m² buildings where two independent instruments agree — roofclf, a per-building size and reflectance classifier, and SPPI, a zero-training spectral index. Requiring agreement raises measured precision on held-out hand-mapped quadrats, at the cost of recall. This is a floor and it is known to be one: OpenStreetMap coverage is incomplete and uneven, and the small-PV term only applies inside the checked cells.
Best estimate this project's own pick
The hand-mapped population plus the satellite model's own ≥400 m² detections, precision-calibrated and recall-corrected, plus the roofclf per-building density estimate inside the same checked cells. The model's detections and the OSM mapping overlap heavily, so the overlap is removed rather than added twice (see the next section). This is the highest number the project defends; it is still incomplete, because the small-PV term covers only a fraction of national buildings.
How double counting is avoided the mapped and the detected are largely the same installations+

OpenStreetMap and the satellite detector are looking at the same country, so naively adding them would count many installations twice. Each model detection carries the identity of the OSM feature it matched, if any: 0 of the 0 hand-mapped installations were also found by the model, and those detections already carry the OSM geometry. The Best estimate therefore adds only the 0 MWp of hand-mapped PV the model never found, on top of the model's own recall-corrected total.

Where the small-PV numbers apply reading the dashed and dotted outlines on the map+

Installations under 400 m² are below the satellite detector's practical floor at 10 m resolution, so they are estimated per building instead. The calibrated estimate used in both tiers is only reported inside the dashed outline: the 0 cells whose building density falls inside the range covered by the hand-mapped calibration quadrats.

Outside that outline, Verified still reads zero — not because small solar is absent there, but because there is no calibration evidence either way. Best adds one more thing in those cells (the dotted outline, 0 cells, 0 MWp nationally): buildings where roofclf and SPPI still independently agree, priced with the same coverage ratios measured on the calibrated cells. This exists because a manual imagery check of that population turned out to be blocked by reference imagery too old to confirm recently-installed panels — agreement between two differently-built detectors is the fallback evidence standard, not a replacement for looking. Every calibration quadrat behind that coverage ratio sits inside the dashed outline, so this is a genuine extrapolation to a less dense, more rural building population, not an interpolation — treat it as the more speculative half of Best, not as validated as anything inside the dashed line.

Data & methods what's under the hood+

Imagery is Sentinel-2 L2A, dry-season composites. Hand-mapped PV is the national OpenStreetMap solar pull, areas measured geodesically, converted at 0.18 kWp/m² for rooftop module area and the land constant for everything else. Detections ≥400 m² come from a TerraMind geospatial foundation model fine-tuned for segmentation, trained on Germany and inferred on this AOI, then precision-calibrated and recall-corrected. Sub-400 m² capacity comes from two per-building instruments (roofclf, a size and reflectance classifier; SPPI, a zero-training spectral index) trained and validated on hand-mapped 1 km² quadrats, restricted to cells whose building density matches those quadrats. Building footprints are VIDA Open Buildings. Full derivations and caveats live in this project's methodology notes (docs/methods/density.md).
