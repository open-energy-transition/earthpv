# Quality levels: Gold, Silver and Bronze

Every country atlas carries a calibration badge in its corner: **Gold**, **Silver** or **Bronze**. It says how far the atlas's figures have been checked, against installations mapped by hand and by people looking at what the model found, not how much solar a country has. The levels add up: Silver needs everything Bronze asks for, and Gold everything Silver asks for. A country shows the highest level whose every criterion it meets.

Near its end, below the panel orientation, every atlas also carries a checklist, *What has been checked so far, and what is still open*: each step below and the two reviews, marked done, under way or not yet, with the numbers behind it and the level that needs it. It is read from the country's records (review verdicts, declarations, the checks the pipeline ran) and refreshed whenever the page is patched, so it can be ahead of the badge: a step done but not yet recorded shows as under way.

## Bronze: every published atlas

| Criterion | What it means | Where it is set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Model trained on the country's own map** | The segmentation model is fine-tuned on the country's own OpenStreetMap solar, and the shipped checkpoint is whichever of that local model and a general one finds more mapped installations in a held-out region chosen before training; a tie goes to the local model. Zambia, run before this rule, uses a general model. | `scripts/pick_val_region.py`, `scripts/compare_local_vs_v5.py` |
| **Imagery over every populated cell** | A dry-season Sentinel-2 composite over every 0.1&deg; cell with at least 1,000 buildings, plus every cell holding mapped solar. Cells nobody imaged are hatched on the map, so bare land means imaged with nothing found. | `earthpv compose` |
| **Hand-mapped solar counted once** | <em>Verified</em> is OpenStreetMap's own solar, clipped to the border and dissolved so a plant outline and the panels mapped inside it count once. | `scripts/prepare_national_osm_solar.py` |
| **Model outlines priced by measured precision** | <em>Best</em> adds the model's outlines of about 400 m&sup2; and up, each weighted by the share of outlines of its size and placement that turned out to be real. Without a local recall reference this is a floor. | `configs/calibration/<aoi>_candidate_precision.yaml` |
| **Very large outlines counted only once checked** | An outline above 100,000 m&sup2; (10 ha) is a blob: 10 m pixels merge everything bright and touching into one shape, so it is as often a sheet of false alarms as a solar park the map does not have yet. A blob counts towards capacity only once it is mapped in OpenStreetMap or a person has judged it solar on the Sentinel-2 imagery the model saw; until then it is an unchecked lead. | `density.load_oversize_admission`, `scripts/oversize_review.py` |
| **Plausibility gate** | Before publication each region's ground-mount to rooftop ratio and its single-cell concentration are checked; a failure is looked at and stated on the page. | `earthpv check-density` |
| **Mapping leads drawn up** | The model's outlines more than 100 m from any solar installation in OpenStreetMap. Mappers are offered only those a person has judged solar on the country's review pages, in one file, largest first; an outline judged not solar is dropped. Leads under 1,000 m&sup2; on ground steeper than 15&deg; are left out (shaded slopes and cliff edges read like panels); larger ones on steep ground stay, marked <code>steep_review</code>, because a solar farm can stand on a mountainside, unless the ground is steeper than 30&deg;, where a lead of any size is left out. France's audited lead release predates this rule. | `scripts/release_leads.py` |

## Silver: Bronze, plus local ground truth and leads mapped to localize the model

| Criterion | What it means | Where it is set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Local ground truth** | The model's precision table is fitted against the country's own mapped installations as a recall reference, or the country has calibration areas in use where every visible panel is mapped (Rule 1). | `atlas.derive_validation_score` |
| **Leads mapped to localize the model** | The mapping flywheel has turned at least once: people have checked a third of a lead file's leads of 1 ha and up, largest first, reviewing them in JOSM and mapping the real installations into OpenStreetMap, and a third of its very large outlines, judged on Sentinel-2 or mapped; and the localized segmentation model has been trained again on the country's OpenStreetMap solar, now holding that mapping. Until this is recorded the atlas shows Bronze, whatever its calibration earns. | `validation.retrained_on_leads` in `configs/atlas/<aoi>.yaml` |

## Gold: Silver, plus installations below 400 m&sup2; checked by people, and glint measured from space

| Criterion | What it means | Where it is set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Installations below 400 m&sup2; calibrated** | An estimate below the 400 m&sup2; floor, where 10 m pixels cannot outline an array: the rooftop classifier priced by a coverage ratio fitted on calibration areas mapped panel by panel, its interval from resampling those areas, or a legally complete national register. | `roofclf`, `sub400_capacity.py` |
| **The small-installation estimate holds up** | That estimate survives the check against its own ground truth: leaving each mapped area out in turn, or the register's municipal totals. | owner judgement, `validation.score` |
| **The rooftop classifier checked by people** | A person has checked the classifier's results on the national scoring behind the page, in two ways. <em>Random validation:</em> cells drawn at random from the whole country, away from the calibration areas, are reviewed on imagery and the real and false detections logged, because calibration areas are chosen on purpose and a classifier that does well there may not do well elsewhere. <em>Point data inspection:</em> the classifier's score for every building, exported as points, is looked through on a map for where it fires and where it stays silent. Until both are recorded the atlas shows Silver. | `scripts/tile_roofclf_detections_geojson.py`, `results/roofclf_random_validation_log.csv`, `scripts/export_roofclf_points.py`; owner judgement, `validation.score` |
| **Solar glint measured on the large installations** | Sentinel-2 catches the sun's reflection off a panel on the few dates when sun, panel and satellite line up: a check from space that is independent of the model and of the mappers, and the only one that measures how the panels are tilted and turned. A glint survey has read at least ten clear scenes over a census or a random draw of the installations of 1 ha and more mapped in OpenStreetMap, which carry most of the capacity: at least a third of them, never fewer than 100 (all of them where a country has fewer), and 1,000 where a third would be more. Whether a panel pose then fits depends on latitude, season and tilt, so it is reported, not required. Until this is recorded the atlas shows Silver. | `scripts/glint_pose_survey.py`, `scripts/glint_coverage.py`; `validation.glint_survey` in `configs/atlas/<aoi>.yaml` |

## When a lead counts as checked

A lead is checked when a person has looked at it and either mapped the installation in OpenStreetMap or found it not solar. **A check EarthPV uses rests on imagery it may use for that**: the Sentinel-2 imagery the model saw, free Copernicus data, or the high-resolution imagery of an OpenStreetMap editor used for what its licence allows, tracing into OpenStreetMap, so that what EarthPV takes from it is the mapped installation. Since 20 November 2026 no verdict on EarthPV's own figures is drawn from Google, Bing, Esri or Mapbox imagery; the rooftop classifier's random-cell check under Gold still shows Esri and Mapbox and has yet to move to imagery or mapping that allows it.

- **The large installations, 1 to 10 ha**, are reviewed in [JOSM](contribute/review.md#the-large-installation-review-review-in-josm-while-mapping) while mapping: the real ones are mapped into OpenStreetMap and from then on count from the map at their real shape; a lead with no solar is marked so it leaves the review, and keeps the share its size class turns out real. Rooftop leads in towns and leads beside roads deserve the same look as the rest: dark asphalt junctions and shadows are the model's commonest false alarms. Leads marked <code>steep_review</code> need a look at the terrain as well.
- **The very large outlines, above 10 ha**, are judged on the country's [solar farm review](contribute/review.md#the-solar-farm-review-judge-on-sentinel-2), three Sentinel-2 pictures of the imagery the model saw, which also shows a park built after any high-resolution capture. One judged not solar is dropped from the lead files; one judged solar moves to the country's confirmed-lead file, the only lead file mappers are offered, and is mapped from there: map the panels or the park's outline, never the blob. What 10 m pixels cannot settle is left unsure, or mapped.

Verdicts given on the review pages before 20 November 2026, which showed Esri World Imagery beside Sentinel-2, stand as they were given; the checklist on each atlas names them as given on the earlier review page. The [lead mapping guide](contribute/leads.md) walks through mapping in JOSM.

## How much to check: a third of the large leads

Only the leads of 1 ha and up count, and the very large outlines above 10 ha: they are the outlines of the two reviews, the large ones reviewed in JOSM and the very large ones judged on Sentinel-2. Smaller leads reach neither, so they are not counted. Checking everything does not pay: where people worked through a whole ranked lead file, the top third held more than two thirds of the leads they went on to map in Pakistan (385 of v3's 4,435 leads of July 2026) and nearly nine in ten in Yemen (246 of 2,259 in a week of October 2026), and below it only 2 to 4% of leads were real. So a third of the large leads and a third of the very large outlines, largest first, checked and the real ones mapped, is what a retrain needs. A maintainer records it as `validation.retrained_on_leads`, naming the lead file, the shares checked and the model trained on the result. It needs doing once: it does not lapse when a new lead file is made.

## The levels follow the mapping flywheel

The levels say how far a country has come round the [mapping flywheel](how-it-works.md#workflow), the loop in which the model and the people mapping solar in OpenStreetMap improve each other's work.

- **Bronze: the wheel is set up.** A model trained on the country's own OpenStreetMap solar detects arrays from space (station 1), and the atlas is published with its mapping leads and the areas to map (station 2).
- **Silver: the wheel has turned once.** People check a third of the large leads, reviewing them in JOSM and mapping the real installations, the very largest judged on Sentinel-2 (station 3), and the model is retrained on what they mapped (station 6), so its next leads come from a model that has learned the country's own false alarms and missed arrays. The figures are checked against local ground truth: mapped installations, complete calibration areas (station 4) or a register (station 5).
- **Gold: the wheel reaches below the floor.** Installations under 400 m&sup2; are too small for the model to outline, so no lead finds them. Calibration areas mapped panel by panel (station 4), or a legally complete register (station 5), are what the small-installation estimate is fitted on and checked against. People then check the rooftop classifier's results directly, on cells drawn at random and on its score for every building, and solar glint, caught from space, corroborates the large installations and measures how their panels are mounted.

Each further turn adds labels the model learns from and areas the estimates are fitted on, so a country moves up by being mapped. The first turn counts for good.
